# Demanding workloads, part 2: torch.compile TRAINING step (2026-07-19)

Second installment of the demanding-workloads series. Part 1
([`DEMANDING_WORKLOADS_1_CUBLAS_INDUCTOR_2026-07-12.md`](DEMANDING_WORKLOADS_1_CUBLAS_INDUCTOR_2026-07-12.md))
measured inference forward only. This part is the harder subject: a full
training step, so the compiled graph gains a **backward pass** (Inductor
fuses `*_backward`-flavored kernels through the AOTAutograd joint graph) and

digest ranking on many more units, `kernel_b` renames between eager and
compiled backward, and bookkeeping tagging on autograd/Dynamo spans that
part 1 never exercised.

## The workload and the compile-scope decision

2-layer MLP (1024 → 2048 → 1024) with LayerNorm + GELU between the linears,
batch 256, MSE loss (regression target of the same 1024-wide shape — the
natural loss for this shape, and it keeps the reduction kernel distinct from
part 1's softmax-sum rather than repeating it), `AdamW(foreach=True)`. One
full step = forward, loss, backward, `optimizer.step`, `zero_grad`. 5 warmup
steps (compilation + autotuning) run **outside** the profiled region; 8
steady-state steps are profiled/traced.

**`--mode compile` compiles the model only** (`torch.compile(model)`), not
the whole step function — measured before deciding, not assumed. A probe
(`torch._dynamo.explain` on the full `forward+backward+opt.step` function)
showed two real obstacles to compiling the whole step:

1. Dynamo graph-breaks at `loss.backward()` itself
   (`Unsupported Tensor.backward() call — Dynamo currently does not support
   tracing Tensor.backward() when trace_autograd_ops is off`), splitting the
   step into two separately-compiled graphs anyway.
2. The second graph Dynamo produces from that break is `optimizer.step()`'s
   `_foreach_*` sequence — which means compiling the whole step would sweep
   the optimizer into Inductor too, contaminating exactly the
   "eager `_foreach_*` ops" surface this study wants to observe cleanly.

Compiling just the model still gets Inductor-compiled forward **and**
backward kernels (AOTAutograd emits the backward joint graph at compile time
regardless of whether the `.backward()` call site itself is traced), while
leaving `opt.step()` genuinely eager, unchanged, in **both** modes — a
clean, single-axis A/B. Confirmed after the fact: the `multi_tensor_apply_kernel`
GPU kernels are present, and closely matched in count/shape, in both traces.

## Captures

| capture | bytes | units |
|---|--:|--:|
| `eager_train.torch-trace.json` (Kineto, CPU+CUDA, 8 steps) | 753,664 | 85 |
| `compiled_train.torch-trace.json` (same) | 622,433 | 83 |
| `train_triton.ncu-rep` (`ncu -k "regex:triton" -c 24 --set full`) | 33,652,401 | 24 launches / 6 distinct kernel names |

`regex:triton` did catch backward kernels, confirmed by name, not assumed:
two of the six distinct kernel names are literally
`triton_red_fused_gelu_gelu_backward_native_layer_norm_native_layer_norm_backward_2`
and `..._backward_3` — Inductor's autogenerated name embeds **both** the
forward op names (`gelu`, `native_layer_norm`) **and** their `_backward`
counterparts in one fused kernel. That is AOTAutograd's activation
recomputation showing up directly in the kernel name: rather than saving the
GELU/LayerNorm activations for backward, the compiled backward kernel cheaply
**recomputes** them inline alongside computing their gradients — a real,
otherwise-invisible architectural fact the digest surfaces for free.

**Capture gotcha (new, not in part 1):** ncu logged two separate
`Connected to process` events during this single script invocation. Verified,
not assumed: re-running with `TORCHINDUCTOR_COMPILE_THREADS=1` collapses it
to one connect. Cause is Inductor's default parallel compile-worker pool —
one worker briefly executes/benchmarks a candidate kernel under ncu's
default "attach to child processes too" behavior before the main process
takes over. All 24 launches were still correctly attributed in the final
report (no data loss); this is a capture-log curiosity worth knowing about,
not a correctness problem.

## What the digests showed

### 1. The hottest unit really is a backward kernel, and it is memory-bound — like part 1's forward fusion, but less extremely

`summarize_report` on the ncu-rep report ranks its top-5 units by
`duration_us`; **all five are separate launches of the same kernel**,
`triton_red_fused_gelu_gelu_backward_native_layer_norm_native_layer_norm_backward_2`
(~32.7–32.9 µs each — real launch-to-launch measurement noise, not
duplication). `get_metrics` on it:

| metric | backward_2 (input-grad path) | backward_3 (weight-grad path) | forward `fused_gelu_native_layer_norm_0` |
|---|--:|--:|--:|
| dram_pct_peak | 83–92 | 77.1 | 50.9 |
| compute_pct_peak | 36–40 | 18.0 | 48.2 |
| achieved_occupancy | 48.5–60.5 | 44.7 | **85.2** |
| theoretical_occupancy | 66.7 | 66.7 | **100.0** |
| registers_per_thread | 56–59 | 64 | 40 |
| l1_hit_rate / l2_hit_rate | 20–49 / 54–71 | **3.2 / 2.1** | 44.9 / 55.5 |
| grid_size | 128 | 32 | 128 |

Both backward kernels are memory-bound (dram_pct_peak clearly dominant), the
same conclusion part 1 reached for the compiled forward's elementwise chain
(92.7% dram / 7.9% compute) — but *less* extremely so: these kernels also do
real reduction arithmetic (LayerNorm backward's mean/variance-gradient sums),
so compute_pct is meaningfully non-trivial (18–40%, not ~8%). The
weight-gradient kernel (`backward_3`) is the more pathological of the two —
its L1/L2 hit rates (3.2% / 2.1%) are far worse than its sibling's, consistent
with scattered gradient-accumulation writes across only 32 blocks. Contrast
with the **forward** fusion: near-ideal 85.2%-of-100%-theoretical occupancy,
balanced compute/memory — the backward fusion trades occupancy down
(44.7–60.5% of a lower 66.7% ceiling) for a bigger register footprint
(56–64 vs 40 regs/thread), the same tensor-core-style occupancy/register
trade part 1 found on **vendor** GEMM kernels, reproduced here on Inductor's
**own** generated backward kernels.

### 2. `compare_metrics` + `kernel_b`: a single-kernel delta got the SIGN wrong

Naive 1:1 rename compare, eager's `GammaBetaBackwardCUDAKernelTemplate`
(LayerNorm weight-grad kernel, avg 9.27 µs) → compiled's `backward_2` via
`kernel_b` (avg 18.64 µs): **`avg_time_us` delta +101%** — reads as Inductor
making this op *slower*. But `backward_2` is not `GammaBetaBackward`'s
replacement — it, plus its sibling `backward_3`, are the fused replacement
for **three** separate eager kernels together:

| | eager (3 kernels) | compiled (2 kernels) |
|---|--:|--:|
| `GeluBackwardCUDAKernelImpl` | avg 14.98 µs | — |
| `layer_norm_grad_input_kernel_vectorized` | avg 9.72 µs | — |
| `GammaBetaBackwardCUDAKernelTemplate` | avg 9.27 µs | — |
| `backward_2` | — | avg 18.64 µs |
| `backward_3` | — | avg 11.14 µs |
| **sum per step** | **33.97 µs, 3 launches** | **29.79 µs, 2 launches** |

The honest aggregate is **−12.3%**, plus one fewer kernel launch. This is
part 1's own caveat ("single-kernel deltas understate/overstate — unit
equivalence is a judgment call") sharpened to its worst case: here a
literal, individually-correct `compare_metrics` call reports the **opposite
sign** of the true fusion effect if read in isolation. The tool did exactly
what it promises (an honest delta between the two named units); the risk is
entirely in an agent trusting one `kernel_b` pairing as "the" before/after
without checking whether the fused unit absorbed more than its named
counterpart. Worth a doc callout, not a tool change — `compare_metrics`
cannot know the fusion topology; only `list_kernels` context can reveal it.

A second, more structural mismatch: eager `MseLossBackward0` (avg 202.5 µs,
one of ~a dozen sibling `autograd::engine::evaluate_function: X` spans) →
compiled `CompiledFunctionBackward` (avg 458.5 µs, the **entire** backward
graph collapsed into one span) via `kernel_b`: +126%. But eager backward has
**no single span that covers "the whole backward"** — the autograd engine
calls each node as a flat sibling, never nested under one wrapping span — so
there is structurally no honest 1:1 eager unit to put on this comparison at
all; `CompiledFunctionBackward` is a genuine many-to-one collapse. Reported
as found: an honest structural fact about how eager vs. compiled backward
graphs are shaped, not a bug in the digest.

### 3. `_foreach_*` optimizer ops: the operation identity and the GPU kernel identity live in different layers

The seven distinct `_foreach_*` calls AdamW makes per step
(`aten::_foreach_add_`, `_foreach_mul_`, `_foreach_lerp_`, `_foreach_addcmul_`,
`_foreach_sqrt`, `_foreach_div_`, `_foreach_addcdiv_`) are each individually
tiny CPU-side `framework_op` units (100–400 µs total over 8 calls,
`get_metrics("aten::_foreach_addcdiv_")` → `avg_time_us: 18.1`). The **GPU**
kernels those dispatch to are *all* the same generic template,
`multi_tensor_apply_kernel<TensorListMetadata<N>, ...Functor...>` — 7
distinct `gpu_kernel` units in the trace, distinguished only by their (also
huge, 400+ char) template-argument tail, not by an operation-level name. An
agent asking "which GPU kernel is `_foreach_addcdiv_`?" cannot get that
answer from the kernel name alone; the semantic identity is CPU-side only,
the GPU-side identity is structural (Functor type) only, and they're
correlated by launch order/timing, not by name. Confirmed identical in
structure across eager and compiled traces, exactly as the compile-scope
decision intended.

Cross-report timing: `Optimizer.step#AdamW.step [gpu_user_annotation]` (the
whole optimizer span) is **14.9% faster** in the compiled run (avg 921.8 µs)
than eager (avg 1083.3 µs) — even though the optimizer's own kernels are
byte-for-byte the same eager code in both modes. Reported as measured, not
explained with confidence: plausibly less launch-queue contention ahead of
it (fewer, larger fused forward/backward kernels vs. eager's longer chain of
small launches), but this run did not isolate the cause.

### 4. Bookkeeping tagging: the known mechanism reproduces, plus a new gap

Part 1's collision-tag mechanism reproduces exactly on a training workload:
`triton_red_fused_gelu_native_layer_norm_0` appears as both
`... [cpu_op]` (the CPU launch span) and `... [kernel]` (the GPU kernel) —
same name, different cat, tag added to stay unique. New in this run:
`Optimizer.step#AdamW.step` collides the same way across `[user_annotation]`
(CPU) and `[gpu_user_annotation]` (GPU-correlated) — a new cat pair hitting
the same mechanism.

**New, awkward finding:** Dynamo/AOTDispatcher's *own* compile-time
bookkeeping spans — `Torch-Compiled Region: 0/0`, `CompiledFunction`,
`TorchDynamo Cache Lookup`, `AOTDispatcher Runtime Wrapper Prologue`,
`Pregraph bytecode` — all carry `cat: "cpu_op"`, collide with nothing, and
are not in the digest's bookkeeping-cat set (`{"Trace", "overhead"}`). They
get **no discount tag at all**, and two of them land directly in the
compiled trace's own `summarize_report` top-5 (`Torch-Compiled Region: 0/0`
avg 691.6 µs, `CompiledFunction` avg 561.6 µs) — indistinguishable from real
compute by the digest's own rules. An agent has to know Dynamo's vocabulary
by name to discount these. Recorded as a v1.3 design question (should the
chrome-trace backend's bookkeeping-cat set grow, or is this Dynamo-specific
enough to need its own tag?), mirroring part 1's `short_name` question —
not silently changed here. (Two `## Call CompiledFxGraph <hash> ##` spans
also appear, one per step for the forward graph and one for the backward
graph, at 478.4 µs and 333.9 µs avg respectively — a clean confirmation that
compiling the model alone really does produce two separately-compiled FX
graphs, forward and backward, exactly as the compile-scope decision assumed.)

### 5. A harder ambiguity mode than part 1: multi-launch capture windows make `#index` mandatory, not optional

Part 1's ncu examples needed no disambiguation — each named kernel appeared
once in its capture window. Here, `-c 24` spans **multiple training steps**
of the same six kernel names, so every `get_metrics` call by bare name on
the ncu report is ambiguous by construction:

```
unit '...backward_2' is ambiguous (16 matches). Disambiguate with
'#<index>' from list_kernels. Available: #0 ..., #1 ..., ...
```

`#5` (the first observed launch) resolves it cleanly — the mechanism works
exactly as designed — but this is worth flagging as a *harder* case than
part 1's: on any ncu capture spanning more than one training step,
`#index` addressing is the **default requirement**, not an edge case an
agent might get lucky and avoid.

### 6. Name payload, worse than part 1

53–57% of `list_kernels`' payload bytes are kernel *names*, not data — a
single mangled ATen kernel
(`at::native::elementwise_kernel<128, 2, ...mse_backward_cuda_kernel...>`)
is **938 characters by itself**. Part 1 found 51% of its payload was two
kernel names combined; here one name alone gets nearly to that same share.
Same honest framing as part 1: the digest is not truncating identity, and
that's correct — but the `short_name` design question from part 1 gets more
evidence, not less.

## Carry-forward findings

1. **Backward-kernel diagnosis holds.** The hottest unit in a training
   capture is reliably a fused backward kernel, correctly identified by
   `summarize_report`/`list_kernels` with no source access, and its
   memory-bound/less-extreme-than-forward character reads straight off
   `get_metrics`' default core set — the same closed-source-diagnosis thesis
   part 1 proved on cuBLAS now holds on Inductor's own backward output.
2. **The sharpest new risk this series has found:** a single, individually
   correct `compare_metrics` call can report the *wrong sign* of a fusion's
   real effect when the compiled side absorbed more ops than the named
   eager unit. Not a tool defect — `list_kernels` context is what catches
   it — but worth a usage-doc callout since it's the kind of mistake an
   agent chasing one `kernel_b` pairing would not notice on its own.
3. **Two independent tag gaps identified across the series now:** part 1's
   `short_name` question (confirmed worse here, 53–57% name payload) and
   this run's new one — Dynamo/AOTDispatcher's own bookkeeping spans
   (`Torch-Compiled Region`, `CompiledFunction`, etc.) are untagged and can
   land in `summarize_report`'s top-N.
4. **`_foreach_*` operator identity and GPU kernel identity live in
   different layers of the same trace** — correlatable by an agent, but not
   nameable as a single unit today.
5. **Multi-step ncu captures make `#index` addressing the default
   requirement**, not a fallback for rare collisions — every kernel name in
   a `-c N` window spanning more than one training step repeats.

## Limitations

- No eager-mode ncu capture was taken — eager mode dispatches pure ATen
  kernels with no `triton`-named units, so an eager run would not match the
  `-k "regex:triton"` filter the task specified; a hardware-counter-level
  eager-vs-compiled comparison was therefore done via the two chrome traces'
  timing metrics only (`compare_metrics`), not ncu counters on both sides.
  This was a scope choice, not a failed capture.
- The `_foreach_*` → `multi_tensor_apply_kernel` GPU-kernel mapping in
  finding 3 is described structurally (identical kernel count/shape across
  eager and compiled); no per-op 1:1 name correlation was attempted, because
  the digest genuinely cannot provide one (see finding 3 itself).
- The "compiled `Optimizer.step` is 14.9% faster despite identical optimizer
  code" observation in finding 3 is reported as measured, not causally
  explained — this run did not isolate why.
- Raw trace/`.ncu-rep` artifacts are **not committed** to this repo: both
  chrome traces exceed the ~500 KB per-artifact guidance (622–754 KB), and
  the `.ncu-rep` is 33.65 MB — all three stay uncommitted, matching part 1's
  precedent and `workloads/README.md`'s existing gitignore rationale
  (regenerable, large/binary). Regenerate with:

```bash
# traces (needs the vllm-024 torch env, or any CUDA torch 2.12+cu132/triton 3.7.1)
python workloads/torch_train_bench.py --mode eager   --trace eager_train.torch-trace.json
python workloads/torch_train_bench.py --mode compile --trace compiled_train.torch-trace.json

# ncu (forward AND backward fused kernels; -k regex:triton misses nothing —
# both `triton_red_fused_gelu_native_layer_norm_0` (forward) and
# `triton_red_fused_gelu_gelu_backward_..._backward_{2,3}` (backward) match)
ncu -k "regex:triton" -c 24 --set full -f -o train_triton \
    python workloads/torch_train_bench.py --mode compile
```

Then digest **through the perfdigest MCP** (never read the raw report into
the agent's context):

```text
summarize_report(report_ref="compiled_train.torch-trace.json", format="torch-trace", top_n=5)
list_kernels(report_ref="train_triton.ncu-rep", format="ncu-rep")
get_metrics(report_ref="train_triton.ncu-rep", format="ncu-rep", kernel="#5")
compare_metrics(report_a="eager_train.torch-trace.json", report_b="compiled_train.torch-trace.json",
                 format="torch-trace", kernel="GammaBetaBackwardCUDAKernelTemplate",
                 kernel_b="triton_red_fused_gelu_gelu_backward_native_layer_norm_native_layer_norm_backward_2 [kernel]")
```
